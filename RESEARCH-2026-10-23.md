## Electoral prospects, kept separate from similarity

“Promising” here means electoral viability, not desirability. No party receives a scoring bonus or penalty for polling strength.

- **Leading contenders:** Yashar and Likud. B’Yachad, Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu, Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism–Zehut, Shas, UTJ, Joint List and Ra’am also have regular above-threshold representation in the reviewed polls.
- **Borderline:** Reservists–Economic. Channel 13 puts it above the threshold while Channel 12 does not. It is a plausible entrant, not an established winner.
- **Weak polling, still running:** Amcha Israel and Haredi Public. Their leaders resisted pressure to withdraw. Noam also insists on continuing despite below-threshold surveys. “Non-starter” would overstate what polls establish; these are labelled below threshold, not eliminated.
- **Withdrawn:** Blue and White. It is excluded even if an older poll still lists it.

Sources: [22 October race report, including contrasting Channel 12/13 polls](https://www.timesofisrael.com/people-of-israel-charedi-public-vow-to-stay-in-race-despite-pressure-from-pm-and-allies/); [Noam leader interview, 12 October](https://www.inn.co.il/news/708325); [22 October Noam report](https://www.walla.co.il/news/politics/383958484). ## Limits and maintenance

This is an update, not independent certification of all 300 scores. Retained broad ideological judgments remain interpretations; not every statement has a direct party quotation. New or narrower questions are not automatically assigned the previous question’s score. The combined religious list and parties without detailed platforms have lower confidence.

The existing 0 convention can make a sparsely documented party look artificially close to a neutral respondent. Changing that requires a separate methodology decision (for example, explicit unknowns excluded from scoring); it is disclosed rather than silently changing the formula here. Polling categories are qualitative and should be refreshed if the dataset is used beyond the stated cutoff. No post-election outcome is assumed.

- **Q12 subsidized long-term rental housing** replaces the constitution question. Judicial independence and the specific override question remain.

A statement need not appear in every party’s programme to be worth asking. We do not invent an opposing position merely to create a contrast. These topics can reveal common ground as well as gaps in documentation. No position located means **unknown**, not “doesn’t care.” General education support, parental choice, communal autonomy or housing assistance does not automatically establish support for the precise decentralization or rental-subsidy proposal.

### Changed scoring and display

All old ambiguous zeroes have been migrated to `null` unless replaced by a newly sourced position. The old zero convention did not distinguish genuine neutrality from missing information, so it cannot itself justify retaining a known neutral value. Existing nonzero interpretations on unchanged questions are retained, with the limitations in the original review; this is not a fresh certification of those positions.

Unknowns contribute neither distance nor maximum distance. A party with no known positions on the user’s answered questions has no score. Evidence coverage is the fraction of **answered question weight** for which a party position is known; the UI also shows the unweighted count. A missing position on an especially important question reduces coverage more, but is not counted as disagreement.

Main-ranking eligibility requires at least 50% coverage and known positions on at least `min(5, answeredCount)` questions. This is an explicit editorial display safeguard, not a scientifically calibrated reliability threshold. Sparse results remain visible separately, with no rank and no top-match card. With fewer than five answers, even fully covered results show a caution. Eligible scores are sorted by similarity, then coverage; unranked results are sorted by coverage. Coverage is never presented as an interest, honesty or competence score.

Scores can still compare different issue subsets. A high score based on five topics is not equivalent in evidential breadth to the same score based on twenty. The per-party expandable panels expose every known/unknown position and direct sources for the new topics, so users can inspect that difference. Neither polling status nor the volume of campaign messaging affects the scores. Old answers are not restored under `2026-10-23-v2`.

### Wording decisions across the questionnaire

- Q1 explains political separation as separate governing systems, without adding a requirement for a sovereign Palestinian state or specifying borders. Q2 makes the intended annexation explicit; it does not invent a territorial percentage.
- Q3 consistently means a body **not subordinate to Hamas**, rather than the stronger claim of independence from all Hamas influence. Q4 consistently transfers budgetary and pedagogical authority from the ministry, rather than transferring a larger number of budgets.
- Q5 specifies conscription age and medical fitness; Q6 concerns men legally required to report for military service, not every non-serving Haredi person. These clarify the intended service proposals; existing directional scores are retained.
- Q8 concerns Jewish religious rules, not Jewish cultural identity alone. Q9 specifies judicial review and invalidation rather than ordinary criticism. Q10 specifies overriding a ruling by re-enacting the same law, rather than correcting its defects. Q11 identifies October 7, **2023** and the statutory state inquiry mechanism.
- Q12 concerns economically affordable long-term rentals, not physical accessibility. Q13 explicitly includes coalition membership and outside parliamentary support. Q14 specifies comparable eligibility and current or past service.
- Q15 presents the existing market-policy versus state-services tradeoff explicitly. Q16 retains the spending-versus-tax tradeoff. These remain broad policy bundles, not separate votes on each tax, regulation or spending department.
- Q17 applies to schools with full or partial state funding and the age-appropriate Education Ministry core curriculum, not universities or every adult educational institution. Q18 identifies Arab and Jewish citizens as the comparison groups.
- Q19 names climate mitigation and environmental protection measures and the economic cost bearers. Q20 retains its existing coalition-leadership meaning. Q21 specifies psychological trauma in all languages, and the Russian wording now locates services in the community.

Apart from independently reassessing the split Q7/Q22, existing scores retain their original policy direction. Qualitative terms such as “large parts,” “high priority” and “substantially” express policy direction, not quantified commitments; exact budgets or borders are not inferred. Future changes that require such thresholds must trigger fresh source and score review. Version `2026-10-23-v3` prevents restoration of answers to older wording. Automated validation covers all 22 positions in three languages, source links for the split topics, rendering through the DOM harness, scoring, and rejection of v2 saved answers. Semantic alignment was reviewed manually; automated checks cannot establish translation accuracy.
